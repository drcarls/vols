# Contemporaneous attribution for the modern arc — the triangulation layer

**The rule the whole book runs on:** a price number means nothing until a *contemporaneous* source
says what the market was pricing. For the pre-1914 chapters that source is *Le Temps*, Lansburgh's
*Die Bank*, the *Neue Freie Presse*, and the *Commercial & Financial Chronicle*. The modern cases
need the same — a market move is cause-vs-cover ambiguous (was 1990's equity slide the *war* or the
*recession*?) until a contemporary voice names the driver. This file is the map: for each modern
case, the contemporaneous source that names what was priced, with real quotes where secured and
precise pointers where still to pull.

Banking & Finance* 29(7):1769–1789 / NBER w9609 |

## A note on the Chronicle for the mid-century US cases

The **pre-1914 Chronicle pipeline works** (`fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/cfc/cfc_YYYYMMDD.pdf`
returns real page images — verified on 1911). But that simple filename **does not resolve for 1950**
(FRASER returns S3 *AccessDenied*): by mid-century the Chronicle was reorganized and FRASER stores it
under a different path/segmentation. So the mid-century Chronicle quotes need either FRASER's
later-era title record (the correct filenames) or the *NYT*/ProQuest historical route. Flagged so no
one assumes the 1911 URL scheme carries forward.

## Why this matters (don't skip it)

Without the contemporaneous layer, the modern charts are just suggestive time series. **With it, each
becomes a triangulated claim** — the BoE saying the oil premium collapsed on allied success; Rigobon
& Sack measuring the war factor in oil; the St. Louis Fed naming the energy channel in 2022. The
older cases have the same voices waiting in the *NYT*/*Chronicle*/*Economist*; this file is the
to-do list to make every modern rung as sourced as Agadir 1911 already is.

*Discipline unchanged: quote the contemporaneous source, flag every unverified figure [VERIFY], and
never let a price move stand as evidence of what it was pricing until a contemporary voice says so.*
