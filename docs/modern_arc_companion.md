# Manuscript companion — the modern arc: what the market priced, 1941–2022

The war-risk-premium framework carried forward from Fraktur OCR to the live tape. The same four
tests — discrimination, instrument, exposure-vs-constraint, press attribution — run unchanged from
Agadir 1911 to the reserve freeze of 2022. (Shareable page: see the published Artifact link in the
handoff; this is the durable repo copy.)

**Thesis.** The market prices the probability of a *general* war — and only the risks it could
*survive to collect on*. That single rule sorts every case: it ignores localized wars, discounts
great-power scares it judges unlikely, prices the ones that threaten to go systemic, and falls
silent before the catastrophe it cannot survive.

## The discrimination ladder (ordered by systemic probability)

| Rung | Verdict | Cases |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Existential** | **Un-priceable** | Berlin 1961 (flat through the tank standoff); Cuba 1962 (low *before* the most dangerous days; rose through them). |
| **General & survivable** | **Priced** | Korea 1950 (−13% invasion → Inchon recovery → −6.5% on China → new highs); Agadir 1911 & Munich 1938 (relief rallies); 2022 (energy channel, Brent +34%). |
| **General, judged unlikely** | **Discounted** | Berlin Airlift 1948 (+21% *through* the scare; −1% at the blockade). Pre-nuclear-parity, so priceable — just judged improbable. |
| **Localized** | **Ignored** | Second Balkan 1913; Falklands 1982 — real wars the great markets discounted. |

The premium appears only on the middle rungs and vanishes at both ends, for opposite reasons.

**The relief-rally diagnostic.** Resolution rallies *only* where the war was priced (Munich,
Agadir). The Berlin Airlift's end passed unremarked because the war was never in the price — the
negative control that proves the rally is a signature of prior pricing.

## The freeze lineage — one weapon, three outcomes

| Case | Weapon | Footprint | Outcome |
|------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **1941** | US freeze of Japanese assets + oil embargo | Invisible in the US tape | **Provoked war** (cornered Japan) |
| **1956 · Suez** | US denies IMF access; sterling run | ~$279m reserve drain (pegged FX) | **Forced a retreat** (Britain aborted) |
| **2022** | G7 freezes ~$300bn reserves; SWIFT | The target's market took it; West priced energy | **Punished, didn't halt** |

Provoked, coerced, punished — "finance is a weapon" disciplined: a weapon with *contingent*
effects, not a lever that always pulls the same way. Effect documented in all three; decisiveness
argued case by case.

## The ledger

| Case | Key move | Reading |
|------|---------|---------|
| Pearl Harbor 1941 | −3.8% | Surprise → no scare to price; modest because half-priced and survivable. |
| Berlin Airlift 1948 | +21% | Rallied through the scare; no relief rally on the lift. |
| Korea 1950–51 | −13% / −6.5% | Same war repriced up and down — discrimination *in motion* (keystone). |
| Gulf War 1990–91 | oil +90% → −30% | Priced through **oil**; the inversion when Desert Storm began (stocks +~4%, oil −~30% in a day). |
| Suez 1956 | ~$279m | Documentary. Reserve drain + IMF denial forced the ceasefire — constraint pole. |
| Berlin 1961 | −0.2% | Flat through the standoff — un-priceable. |
| Cuba 1962 | −3.8% | Low preceded peak danger; rose through it — the ceiling. |
| Iraq War 2003 | S&P +9% / oil −$7 | Rigobon–Sack's own case; the "war factor" in oil, resolved on invasion. |
| Russia–Ukraine 2022 | Brent +34% | Equity up on invasion day; priced as energy shock, not systemic war; weapon in full. |

**The oil lineage (parallel to the freeze lineage).** Gulf 1990 → Iraq 2003 → 2022 — three wars the
market priced through the barrel. *Which instrument carries the risk* is a property of the crisis:
1905 → sovereign bonds (the *fonds russes*); 1911/1956 → the exchange/reserves; the Gulf Wars & 2022
→ **oil**. Sack & Rigobon (2005) measured the 2003 oil "war factor" by the same heteroskedasticity ID
this book uses for 1911.

**The contemporaneous layer.** Each rung needs a contemporary voice naming what was priced — the
BoE Quarterly Bulletin on the 1991 oil-premium collapse, Rigobon–Sack on the 2003 factor, the St.
Louis Fed on the 2022 energy channel; the *NYT*/*Chronicle*/*Economist* pointers for the mid-century
cases are mapped in `docs/modern_contemporaneous_sources.md`. Without it the charts are suggestive
series; with it each is a triangulated claim.

## The discipline

- **Effect vs. decisiveness.** A price move shows the market *reacted*, not that finance *changed
  the outcome*. "Decisive lever by the actors' own accounts" (Suez), not "finance ended Suez."
- **Charted vs. cited.** Tape series are reproducible; Suez reserves and the 2022 Russian-side
  figures carry [VERIFY]. And twice over: the 1948–49 and June-2022 declines were the *cycle*
  (recession; the Fed), not the war.

## Against Kirshner

A *pricing* result, not a preference claim. It **measures** what he asserts — the caution was
selective (only the general-war rungs) and modest — and adds the face his dove frame omits:
finance as the **weapon** (the freeze lineage), contingent, not automatic.
