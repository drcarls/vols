# Modern run — Suez 1956: finance as the binding constraint

The deliberate contrast pair to **1905**. There, the honest verdict was *finance did **not**
constrain France* — Russia's **military** collapse did, and finance was the symptom, not the
cause. Suez is the clean opposite pole: the case where **finance unambiguously *did* constrain**,
and by the participants' own testimony was the *decisive* lever. It anchors the constrained end of
the exposure → lever → constraint spectrum, and it keeps the whole taxonomy honest by showing what
a *real* constraint looks like, so the label is not applied loosely elsewhere.

## What happened

Britain, France, and Israel moved militarily against Nasser's Egypt (Sinai invasion 29 Oct 1956;
Anglo-French landings at Port Said 5 Nov). Militarily the operation was succeeding. It was stopped
by **money**:

| Date | Event | The financial fact |
|------|-------|--------------------|
| **5 Nov 1956** | Port Said landings | A **run on sterling**; UK gold/dollar reserves fell **~$279m in November** — ~15% in a month — toward the ~$2bn danger line *[VERIFY]* |
| **6 Nov 1956** | **Ceasefire** | The US (Treasury Secretary **George Humphrey**) **blocked British access to IMF drawing and US support** until Britain committed to a ceasefire and withdrawal |
| **3 Dec 1956** | Withdrawal announced | Once compliance was committed, the block was lifted |
| **10 Dec 1956** | **IMF package released** | Britain drew **~$561m** from the IMF and arranged a **~$739m standby** (~$1.3bn total); the Ex-Im line and **US oil** were restored *[VERIFY vs Boughton/IMF, Kunz]* |

## Why it is the constraint case

Three features make Suez the textbook binding constraint — each absent in 1905:

1. **The lever was deliberate and identifiable.** Not defensive capital flight (the 1911
   ambiguity) but a *policy*: the US Treasury chose to withhold IMF drawing rights, standby
   support, and emergency oil, and to relent only on withdrawal. Cause and agent are named.
2. **The counterfactual is tight.** Britain had the *military* means to continue; what it lacked
   was the *reserves* to defend sterling through a prolonged operation without US help. Remove the
   financial squeeze and the operation plausibly continues — the hallmark of a binding constraint,
   not a mere symptom.
3. **The participants said so.** **Macmillan** — Chancellor, "first in, first out" — swung from
   hawk to dove as the reserves drained; the Cabinet's own accounts attribute the climbdown
   substantially to the sterling crisis and the US refusal to help. The *stated* cause and the
   *revealed* mechanism agree — the press-triangulation test passed at the level of the decision
   itself.

## The honesty rule still applies

Even here, the withdrawal was **over-determined** — UN condemnation, Commonwealth and domestic
opposition, and Soviet threats all pushed the same way. The claim is not that finance was the
*sole* cause but that it was the **decisive lever by the actors' own accounts**, and that the
financial mechanism is unusually well-documented. Write *"the sterling crisis and US financial
coercion were the decisive lever that forced the withdrawal,"* not *"finance alone ended Suez."*
That is exactly the discipline 1905 taught — applied here to a case that *passes* the test rather
than fails it.

## Where it sits in the book

- The **matched pair with 1905**: *finance did not constrain (1905) / finance did constrain
  (1956)* — the two poles that define the spectrum and prove the framework can tell them apart.
- The **coercive edge, state-wielded (face #3)**, in its cleanest mid-century form — the direct
  ancestor of the **1941 asset freeze** and the **2022 reserve freeze**. Suez is the case where
  the squeeze demonstrably *worked* and *changed the outcome* — the benchmark the other coercion
  cases are measured against.
