# Forward applications — the method on today's confrontations

*Where the instrument built for 1905–1914 points now. Same four questions (discrimination, which
instrument, exposure-vs-weapon, contemporaneous attribution), same discipline (effect vs intent;
present is not binding; anticipation is weak and resolution is strong), same limit (you cannot price
what you will not survive to collect on). This is the afterword's "lessons for today" carried onto
the live board. Charted series are reproducible (Yahoo/FRED); everything forward-looking is
structure, not forecast — the method's whole point is that it refuses to forecast a decision no one
has made.*

---

## China–US over Taiwan — the hardest case, and the one the method most changes

Run the four tests on a Taiwan war and the results cut against the intuitions that dominate the
commentary.

### 1. Discrimination — and why market calm is not reassurance

A war over Taiwan is a candidate for a *general* war — US–China, possibly nuclear — which puts it
near the **top** of the discrimination ladder, in the neighbourhood of Cuba 1962, not the Gulf. The
framework therefore predicts two things at once: the market will be **poor at pricing it in advance**
(the anticipation channel cannot price a decision Beijing has not made), and to the degree it
approaches the existential, it drifts toward **un-priceable** (you cannot fully price a US–China war
you might not collect on). Both mean the same operational warning: **a calm market is not a forecast
of peace over Taiwan.** It is the July-1914 error — mistaking the absence of a priced signal for the
absence of a danger.

The evidence we have is consistent with exactly this. When Nancy Pelosi visited Taiwan in August
2022 and China answered with its largest-ever blockade drills — missiles over the island, a
rehearsal of quarantine — the markets barely moved:

| Instrument | Trough (2–12 Aug) | By 19 Aug |
|---|---:|---:|
| Semiconductors (SOX) | **−3.7%** | +2.0% |
| TSMC ADR | −1.3% | +3.4% |
| Taiwan TAIEX | −1.9% | +2.8% |
| S&P 500 | −0.7% | +4.0% |

The market discounted it — read it as a scare that would stay a drill, and it was right *that time*
(the Balkans/Falklands pattern: a real military event the great markets ignore). But the −3.7%
semis wobble that reversed in a fortnight tells you the market judged **this episode** would stay
below the general threshold. It does **not** tell you the market has priced an actual invasion. If
Beijing decided, the repricing would come at the **resolution**, be violent, and land in the exposed
instruments — not in the ambient calm beforehand. (Data: `war_premia/data/taiwan_pelosi_2022.csv`.)

### 2. Which instrument carries the risk — the silicon shock

For the Gulf and Iran the exposed instrument is oil. For Taiwan it is **semiconductors** — Taiwan
fabricates the overwhelming majority of the world's advanced chips, so a Taiwan war is a *silicon*
shock the way a Gulf war is an *oil* shock. Read the exposed instrument, not the index: the
semiconductor complex (the SOX, TSMC, the firms whose supply chains run through the island), then
the **target's own market** (Taiwan and Chinese equities, the Taiwan dollar and the yuan — the ruble
of this scenario), and then the **financial-weapon instruments** below. A generalist watching the
S&P will see nothing until it is over; the signal, when it comes, will be in chips and in Chinese
assets first.

### 3. Anticipation vs resolution — and the new instrument

The informative moments will be **resolutions**, not the ambient level: an actual blockade declared,
an actual invasion, an actual sanctions package. Until one of those, the market is in its weak
channel, pricing a decision Xi has not taken. And as with Iran 2026, the anticipation channel is now
**partly observable**: prediction markets carry standing Taiwan-invasion and blockade questions, so
one can read the crowd's *probability* directly and watch whether chip equities move with it — the
same decomposition (probability off the prediction market, magnitude off the exposed instrument),
with the same caution that the odds line is thin, manipulable, and can cry wolf.

### 4. Finance as weapon — and the book's "Never Again" engine, running *before* the crisis

This is where the method says the most, and the least obvious thing. The 2022 freezing of Russia's
reserves was the loudest financial-weapon act in a century — and its most important audience was
**Beijing**. China holds a vast dollar reserve and a large Treasury position; it watched a peer's
reserves immobilised overnight, and has been **hardening ever since**: accumulating gold, trimming
Treasury exposure, building CIPS as a SWIFT alternative, advancing the digital yuan.

That is the manuscript's **"Never Again" engine** — each crisis exposes one power's gap, and the
years afterward are spent closing it — playing out *pre-emptively*. Germany, squeezed at Agadir,
spent 1911–1914 on the liquidity reform it *completed the month the war began*; China, watching
Russia squeezed in 2022, is doing its reserve-and-payments reform **in advance of its own crisis**.
The forward implication is the book's implication, dated to now: **the deterrent value of the US
financial weapon over China decays every year China de-risks**, exactly as German liquidity reform
drained the Agadir squeeze of its force by July 1914. A financial lever announced is a financial
lever the target starts building around.

The discipline holds unchanged: the reserve freeze's *effect* on Russia is documented; its
*decisiveness* was not (it punished without halting), and the same caution applies to any assumed
US financial leverage over China — **document the effect, argue the decisiveness, never assume it.**

### 5. The un-priceable ceiling — the limit that matters most here

A full US–China war is close enough to the existential that the market **cannot represent it** —
the Cuba result. The mutual-economic-destruction logic that is supposed to deter it (integrated
supply chains, dollar dependence, the sheer cost) is real, but it is precisely the kind of tail the
market *cannot price*, because there is no surviving branch in which the bearish position pays. So
the absence of a Taiwan premium in today's prices is **not** evidence the risk is low — it is
evidence the instrument has hit its ceiling. Do not outsource the probability of a US–China war to
asset prices; they are structurally silent on it.

## Other situations — the same ladder, quickly

| Situation | Rung / instrument | What the method says |
|---|---|---|
| **US–China trade & tech decoupling** (short of war) | Priced, survivable; instrument = **semis** | The market handles this *well* — chip equities reprice cleanly on each export-control or tariff (a *resolution* each time). This is the strong channel; watch the SOX on announcement days. |
| **North Korea** | Localized; great markets ignore | The Balkans/Falklands pattern — missile-test spikes that fade (cried wolf). Regional assets carry it; global markets shrug. Calm here *is* roughly informative, because it genuinely stays localized. |
| **India–Pakistan** | Localized, nuclear-armed | The great markets discount it; the rupee and regional equity take the damage (Falklands at the belligerent). A localized nuclear war is still *localized* to the pricing markets. |
| **Russia–NATO beyond Ukraine** | Escalation tail toward un-priceable; instrument = **European energy** + the freeze | Finance already the weapon (the 2022 freeze); the energy channel carries Europe's exposure; the direct-war tail sits near the Cuba ceiling — unpriceable, so silence ≠ safety. |
| **A financial / cyber confrontation** | Finance *is* the battlefield | The 2022 apotheosis extended: reserve freezes, SWIFT/CIPS, CBDC competition, rare-earth counter-leverage. The exposure/lever/weapon taxonomy is the whole analysis; effect-vs-decisiveness the whole discipline. |
| **Climate / AI as civilizational tails** | Above the ceiling | The Cuba lesson generalized beyond war: markets cannot price a catastrophe they will not survive to collect on, so their **silence on existential risk is not reassurance**. The single most important transfer of the method outside its home domain. |

## The through-line

Four moves recur on every board above, and they are the book's:

1. **Ask which war it could become** (general → priced or un-priceable; localized → ignored), and
   never read calm as a forecast when the answer is "general."
2. **Find the exposed instrument** — oil, chips, reserves, energy — and watch *it*, not the index.
3. **Wait for the resolution** — markets price a taken decision, not an undecided one; the signal is
   the blockade or the invasion or the sanctions package, dated, not the ambient level.
4. **Track the weapon and its decay** — a financial lever announced is one the target hardens
   against; deterrent value is a wasting asset, and the "Never Again" engine is running in Beijing
   now.

And the one limit, which is also the one warning: **the risks that would end the game are the ones
the market cannot price at all.** On those, prices are not a signal. Somebody still has to be in the
room to ask the money question — the market cannot ask it for you.
