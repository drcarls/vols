# Research agenda — extending the war-risk-premium framework

Where the method built for the pre-1914 crises can travel. The point is not "which wars
were big" but which crises stress the four things this framework does that a standard
price-series study does not.

## What the framework selects for

1. **The discrimination test** — did the market price a *general/systemic* war, or a
   *localized* one, or a *pure diplomatic scare*? (The pre-1914 finding: it priced only the
   crises that could have gone general.)
2. **Which instrument carries the risk** — money market, sovereign bonds, or the exchange.
   The 1905 lesson: sometimes the money market reads null while the risk sits in the bond
   market (the *fonds russes*).
3. **Exposure vs. constraint** — is finance a *symptom*, a *lever*, or the *binding
   constraint*? Most cases are symptom/lever; a few (Suez, 2022) are genuine weapon.
4. **Press triangulation** — a market number means little until the contemporary financial
   press says what it was pricing (cause vs. cover).

A case earns its place by stressing at least two of these — and a *revisited* case (Munich,
the Confederate bonds) is still novel if the contribution is the method, not the event.

## Tier 1 — the strongest fits (start here)

- **Munich / the Sudeten crisis, September 1938 — the interwar Agadir.** A war scare that was
  *faded*: markets slide as war looms, then the **"Munich relief rally"** on Chamberlain's
  return ("peace for our time"), then the real thing in 1939–40. It replays the whole thesis
  (cried wolf → the one time real) with far better data. Money-market + bond + FX + the
  *Economist*/FT and continental press. *Extends the Frey–Kucher–Waldenström bond-barometer
  program with the press-attribution layer.*
- **American Civil War / the Erlanger cotton loan (1863) — bonds as a battle barometer.** The
  purest case that sovereign/quasi-sovereign bonds track military fortunes, and of
  finance-as-diplomacy (King Cotton; the Erlanger loan floated in London/Paris to buy
  recognition). Confederate bond prices abroad moved on Gettysburg/Vicksburg. The bond-market
  twin of the *fonds russes* finding; existing literature (Weidenmier; Willard–Guinnane–Rosen
  on the greenback/gold market) to build on or argue with.
- **The 1871 French indemnity loan — finance as recovery-lever.** The 19th-c. mirror of the
  1906 Russian loan, with France as *debtor*: the wildly over-subscribed Thiers loans let
  France pay the 5-billion-franc indemnity and end the German occupation early. **Lowest-effort
  extension: it runs on the exact Le Temps / Gallica pipeline we already built** (the French
  rente and the loan subscriptions on the Paris Bourse).

## Tier 2 — finance as weapon / constraint (stress-test the discipline)

These are where finance was arguably the *binding* thing, so they test the causal line the
1905 case forced us to draw.

- **French–Belgian occupation of the Ruhr, 1923 — finance as the whole battlefield.**
  Reparations default → occupation → passive resistance → the mark's **hyperinflationary
  death** → the 1924 **Dawes Plan** (American loans stabilizing Germany). Finance is
  simultaneously the weapon (reparations, occupation) and the rescue (Dawes) — the richest
  interwar case for the exposure/lever/constraint taxonomy. German + French press; the mark
  and German bond series.
- **Suez, 1956 — the clean constraint case.** The US used the threat to sterling and IMF
  access to *force* Britain's withdrawal. If 1905 is "finance did **not** constrain," Suez is
  "finance unambiguously **did**" — the deliberate contrast pair.
- **Pearl Harbor via the 1941 asset freeze — finance as trigger.** The strong angle is not the
  raid but the **US freeze of Japanese assets + oil embargo (July–Aug 1941)** that helped push
  Japan to war. Suez/2022 lineage.
- **Russia–Ukraine, 2014 & 2022 — the modern apotheosis.** Reserve freeze, SWIFT expulsion,
  CDS blow-out, the ruble's collapse-and-capital-controls rebound. Shows the framework scaling
  from Fraktur OCR to real-time CDS; finance as weapon in full.

## Tier 3 — the nuclear option (the general-war-probability extreme)

- **Berlin Crisis, 1961 (the Checkpoint Charlie tank standoff)** and **the Cuban Missile
  Crisis, 1962.** Run them as a **pair**: superpower nuclear brinkmanship, the ultimate "cried
  wolf that nearly wasn't." The recurring — and profound — result is how *little* markets move:
  **you cannot price a catastrophe you will not survive to collect on.** That is the limit of
  the discrimination test — markets price survivable/collectable risks, not existential ones —
  and it is a strong closing argument, not a null to apologize for.

## Tier 4 — discrimination-test data points & adjacent cases

- **Falklands, 1982** — a clean *localized-war* entry: sterling barely blinked while Argentina
  (already unravelling in the 1982 debt crisis) took the financial damage. Sits beside the
  Second Balkan War as "a war the great markets ignored."
- **Vietnam–dollar–gold nexus, 1965–71** — *not* Tet as a discrete event (that's the opinion
  turning point), but the guns-and-butter inflation, the **March-1968 London Gold Pool
  collapse**, and the strain that killed Bretton Woods.
- **Boer War, 1899–1902** (Consols; British war finance); **Anschluss / Prague, 1938–39**
  (neutral-market bond prices); **Korea, 1950** (the Wall Street "war scare"); **the Gulf Wars**
  (the oil-and-equity "war premium"). Useful control/comparison points.

## Practical sequencing

- **Cheapest first:** the **1871 indemnity loan** and a **Franco-Prussian rente** trace reuse
  the Le Temps/Gallica pipeline verbatim — a near-drop-in extension.
- **Highest payoff:** **Munich 1938** (best data, direct thesis replay) and the **Erlanger
  bonds** (the bond-barometer structure the *fonds russes* opened).
- **Sharpest contrast:** **Ruhr 1923** and **Suez 1956** to anchor the finance-as-weapon end
  of the exposure→lever→constraint spectrum, keeping the causal discipline honest.

## Positioning (overlap discipline)

What is scarce is not the events but the **discipline applied to them**: the press-triangulation,
the general-vs-localized test, and the exposure-vs-constraint rule. Any case a prior book has
"taken" can still be ours if the contribution is that method and framing rather than the event
selection. De-conflict a specific prior title case-by-case (tag each entry *clear / adjacent /
covered*) once the title is fixed.
