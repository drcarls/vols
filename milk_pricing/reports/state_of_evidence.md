# State of the evidence — what actually got stronger, and what didn't

The control states did **not** make the finding stronger. They made it better characterised, and what they characterised is narrower and more design-dependent than I represented two turns ago.

## The decisive test

Design-free DiD (Walmart minus Aldi within each ZIP, no matching, no comparator pool), with within-state permutation:

| Sample | nB | nW | DiD | t | **Within-state p** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Test states (SC/LA/MS) | 92 | 20 | +$0.468 | 2.13 | **0.153** |
| Control states (TN/NC/AL/GA) | 95 | 157 | −$0.068 | −0.77 | 0.448 |
| All seven | 187 | 177 | +$0.238 | 2.84 | **0.216** |

**The design-free version is not significant even in the states selected for showing the effect.** Everything that survives rests on the matched design.

That matters because the matched design is demonstrably capable of manufacturing significance: in North Carolina it returns +$0.255 at p=0.017 while the same data unmatched returns −$0.026.

## What genuinely got stronger

1. **Aldi is now a validated control retailer.** Null in the control states (p=0.448), null pooled (+$0.014, p=0.535), and null in five of seven states individually. A control that never fires is worth having.
2. **Four out-of-sample states are null.** TN, GA, NC and AL were chosen without reference to the outcome and none shows a design-free effect. The finding is not everywhere, which is real evidence against fishing.
3. **Georgia independently corroborates the memo's federal-layer claim.** Black rural GA sits at 5.8–6.0/cwt and white rural at 5.4–5.6, with 2 of 39 Black ZIPs having any same-floor white comparator. That is the Black Belt argument with numbers, and it is new.

## What got weaker

1. **The pooled claim is gone.** Seven states, design-free: p=0.216.
2. **The three-state claim is design-dependent.** p=0.0073 matched, p=0.153 unmatched.
3. **Louisiana is entirely design-dependent.** Unmatched DiD +$0.020 — nil. It was the one state that cleared alone on the matched test.
4. **My "robust to abandoning the matching" claim was wrong** and is withdrawn.

## What can be said

> In South Carolina, Louisiana and Mississippi, an income- and population-matched comparison of majority-Black to majority-white rural ZIPs finds Walmart milk priced $0.45–$1.09/gal higher, while the same comparison applied to Aldi in the same ZIPs finds no gap. The contrast does not appear in four other Southern states tested. The result depends on the matching specification: an unmatched comparison of the same ZIPs is directionally consistent but not statistically significant.

That last sentence is not optional. It is the first thing an opposing expert will establish, and a memo that states it first is far more credible than one that has it extracted.

## What would make it genuinely stronger

Not more states — the control states already did their job. The weakness is that the estimate hangs on a matching specification with small comparator pools. What fixes that:

1. **More white rural ZIPs per state**, which means either more stores in the file or relaxing "rural" — SC has 10, LA 6, MS 4. That constraint, not the number of states, is what caps the design.
2. **A time series.** The memo notes one is collecting. A price gap that persists across months is far harder to attribute to a specification than a single snapshot, and it removes the different-collection-dates caveat between Walmart and Aldi.
3. **A second control retailer** — Dollar General was priced nationally but without store context. If a third chain also shows null where Walmart is positive, the retailer-specificity argument stops resting on Aldi alone.
