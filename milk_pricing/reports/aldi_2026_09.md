# The September re-collection: one claim proven, one claim broken

**The shop is definitively the Aldi pricing unit, and the milk carve-out reproduces
strongly. But the retailer comparison — described three days ago as "the strongest
evidence in this analysis" — does not hold up. It should be withdrawn as a headline.**

Reproduce: `python3 analysis/aldi_collect.py` then `python3 analysis/aldi_2026_09.py`

---

## Collection

1,886 ZIPs re-pulled 2026-09-16 in a single 29-minute window, **1,867 priced, 0 errors**,
direct to aldi.us with no proxy or credential. Unlike August this run records **shopId**
and the full 26-item dairy basket. 1,469 usable after demographics and state exclusions;
**641 distinct shops**.

## 1. The pricing unit is the shop — now proven, not assumed

The August panel recorded only a `zone`, and treating it as the pricing unit produced a
false published claim (`reports/aldi_comparison.md` §4). The new data settles it:

| candidate unit | units | carrying >1 price | widest spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shop** | 641 | **0 (0%)** | **$0.00** |
| zone (August's field) | 553 | 124 (22%) | $2.26 |
| county | 503 | 162 (32%) | $2.20 |
| state | 12 | 12 (100%) | $2.46 |

**Every one of 641 shops carries exactly one price.** No other candidate comes close.
The correction stands and is now demonstrated rather than inferred.

| **Friendly Farms 2% Reduced Fat, 1 gal** | 1,466 | **46** | 0.575 |

Note the shape: it is *fluid* milk specifically. Shelf-stable and lactose-free variants
sit with the cheeses at 2–3 prices.

## 3. Aldi prices moved between August and September

On 1,320 paired ZIPs: correlation **+0.780**, only **39% identical**, mean change
−$0.029. A separate intraday check found one store's five fluid-milk items all moving
within 30 minutes while its 17 non-milk items held to the cent.

So the series is volatile in exactly the item the analysis depends on. That matters for
what follows.

## 4. The retailer comparison does not replicate robustly

`reports/aldi_comparison.md` reported that in low-income rural ZIPs Walmart's racial gap
is positive while Aldi's is negative, a difference of +24¢. On the same design:

| Aldi series | sample | Walmart gap | Aldi gap | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| August | 292 ZIPs in all three series | +$0.133 | −$0.088 | **+$0.221** |
| September | same 292 ZIPs | +$0.133 | −$0.026 | **+$0.159** |
| September | all 313 available ZIPs | +$0.142 | **+$0.110** | **+$0.032** |

**On like-for-like ZIPs the sign survives** — Aldi is negative in both vintages — but the
magnitude attenuates from 22¢ to 16¢. **On the fuller September sample it collapses to
3¢ and Aldi turns positive.**

### What drives the collapse: fourteen ZIP codes

| September sample | n high-Black | n low-Black | Aldi gap | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZIPs also in August | 138 | 78 | −$0.030 | +$0.175 |
| **+ 14 ZIPs new in September** | 138 | **92** | **+$0.110** | **+$0.032** |

The fourteen additions are all low-Black, **twelve of them in Texas**. Adding fourteen
observations to one cell moves the estimate from +17.5¢ to +3.2¢.

**A result that moves by a factor of five on fourteen observations is not a stable
estimate.** It was never statistically significant — t +0.96 clustered at its strongest
— and it is now not robust either.

## 5. What to do with it

**Withdraw the retailer comparison as a headline.** The claim that it is "the strongest
evidence in this analysis" is not supportable. The defensible version:

> On matched ZIP codes Aldi's racial gradient in low-income rural areas is negative in
> both collection vintages while Walmart's is positive, a difference of 16–22¢/gallon.
> The comparison is directionally consistent but unstable in magnitude: it falls to 3¢
> when fourteen predominantly Texan low-Black ZIPs enter the comparison group, and it
> never reaches significance in any specification.

**What survives untouched:** the pricing-unit finding (§1), the milk carve-out (§2), and
the single-chain Walmart rural gradient in `reports/segmented_rural.md`, which uses no
Aldi data at all.

## 6. Why this happened, and what it says about the earlier result

The August Aldi panel was collected without shop identification and without timestamps,
against a series that moves intraday. It was then used for a difference-in-differences
whose whole value came from the two chains being measured in the same places at the same
time. The re-collection shows the second half of that assumption was weaker than stated.

The general lesson for this project, which has now produced three corrections of the same
shape: **a comparison is only as good as the stability of its weakest series, and
stability has to be measured rather than assumed.** The August data could not support
that check, because it recorded neither the pricing unit nor the collection time. The
September data records both.
