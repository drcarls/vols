# The September re-collection: one claim proven, one claim broken

**The shop is definitively the Aldi pricing unit, and the milk carve-out reproduces
strongly. But the retailer comparison — described three days ago as "the strongest
evidence in this analysis" — was an artifact of race-correlated missing data in the
August collection. It should be withdrawn, not qualified.**

Reproduce: `python3 analysis/aldi_collect.py` then `python3 analysis/aldi_2026_09.py`

---

## Collection

1,886 ZIPs re-pulled 2026-09-16 in a single 29-minute window, **1,867 priced, 0 errors**,
direct to aldi.us with no proxy or credential. Unlike August this run records **shopId**
and the full 26-item dairy basket. 1,469 usable after demographics and state exclusions;
**641 distinct shops**.

## 1. The pricing unit is the shop — now proven, not assumed

The August panel recorded only a `zone`, and treating it as the pricing unit produced a
false published claim (`reports/aldi_comparison.md` §4). The new data settles it:

| candidate unit | units | carrying >1 price | widest spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shop** | 641 | **0 (0%)** | **$0.00** |
| zone (August's field) | 553 | 124 (22%) | $2.26 |
| county | 503 | 162 (32%) | $2.20 |
| state | 12 | 12 (100%) | $2.46 |

**Every one of 641 shops carries exactly one price.** No other candidate comes close.
The correction stands and is now demonstrated rather than inferred.

## 2. The milk carve-out reproduces at Aldi, strongly

Across the 26-item dairy basket, by number of distinct prices nationally:

| item | n | distinct prices | sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Friendly Farms Whole Milk, 1 gal** | 1,469 | **47** | 0.582 |
| **Friendly Farms 2% Reduced Fat, 1 gal** | 1,466 | **46** | 0.575 |
| Friendly Farms Vitamin D Whole, 0.5 gal | 1,460 | 34 | 0.412 |
| Friendly Farms 2%, 0.5 gal | 1,458 | 34 | 0.393 |
| Friendly Farms 1% Chocolate, 0.5 gal | 1,298 | 33 | 0.389 |
| Goldhen Large Grade A Eggs, 12 ct | 1,039 | 12 | 0.103 |
| Simply Nature Organic Whole Milk, 64 fl oz | 898 | 3 | 0.045 |

the retail strand and it is independent of any racial analysis.

Note the shape: it is *fluid* milk specifically. Shelf-stable and lactose-free variants
sit with the cheeses at 2–3 prices.

## 3. Aldi prices moved between August and September

On 1,320 paired ZIPs: correlation **+0.780**, only **39% identical**, mean change
−$0.029. A separate intraday check found one store's five fluid-milk items all moving
within 30 minutes while its 17 non-milk items held to the cent.

So the series is volatile in exactly the item the analysis depends on. That matters for
what follows.

## 4. The retailer comparison was an artifact of missing data

`reports/aldi_comparison.md` reported that in low-income rural ZIPs Walmart's racial gap
is positive while Aldi's is negative, a difference of **+24¢** — and called it the
strongest evidence in the analysis. It is not a real difference.

**August did not have a smaller ZIP list. It had the same 1,886 ZIPs and failed to
collect a price for 149 of them.** September, with a more robust parser, recovered all
149 — and those failures were not random:

| | n | mean % Black | mean median income |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Priced in August** | 1,333 | **16.3%** | $69,197 |
| **Missing in August** | 149 | **5.0%** | $78,196 |

By state, the August failure rate was **Texas 20%** (82 ZIPs) and **California 25%**
(63 ZIPs), against 0–3% everywhere else.

So August systematically lost low-Black, higher-income ZIP codes — **precisely the
comparison group the racial gap is measured against**, and concentrated in the two states
that would have supplied most of it.

### The decisive test: hold prices constant, change only the sample

Using the **September prices throughout**, so vintage cannot be the explanation:

| sample | n high-Black | n low-Black | Aldi gap | Walmart gap | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZIPs August managed to price | 138 | 78 | −$0.030 | +$0.144 | **+$0.175** |
| **All ZIPs, August's 149 failures recovered** | 138 | **92** | **+$0.110** | +$0.142 | **+$0.032** |

**The entire result is the sample, not the vintage.** Restricting to the ZIPs August
happened to price inflates the difference more than fivefold. On the complete data,
Aldi's gradient turns positive — the same direction as Walmart's — and the retailer
difference is **+3¢, essentially zero**.

### What this means

**The Aldi contrast should be withdrawn, not merely qualified.** The earlier report
described it as suggestive-but-unstable; that was too generous. The best available
estimate of the Walmart-versus-Aldi difference, on complete and shop-identified data, is
approximately zero. The 16¢–22¢ figures were produced by a comparison group with a fifth
of Texas and a quarter of California missing from it.

**This also removes the answer to the "it's the rural South" objection.** That objection
was the reason the comparison mattered, and the comparison no longer refutes it. The
single-chain Walmart finding in `reports/segmented_rural.md` stands on its own terms but
is once again without a control.

## 6. Why this happened, and what it says about the earlier result

The August Aldi panel was collected without shop identification and without timestamps,
against a series that moves intraday. It was then used for a difference-in-differences
whose whole value came from the two chains being measured in the same places at the same
time. The re-collection shows the second half of that assumption was weaker than stated.

The general lesson, and this project has now produced four corrections of the same shape:
**a result computed on the rows a collection happened to succeed on is only as good as
the randomness of those successes, and that has to be measured rather than assumed.**
Nothing in the August file recorded which ZIPs it had failed on — a missing price and an
unattempted ZIP were indistinguishable. The September collector writes a row for every
ZIP including failures, with an explicit error field, so the same check can be run
directly next time.
