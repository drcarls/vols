# Why is Walmart high where Aldi is compressed?

Short answer: **it isn't, and Aldi isn't compressed.** Both halves of the premise dissolve against the full data, and what's left is a single store.

## Aldi is not compressed

The 538-ZIP sweep gives Aldi a South Carolina range of **$2.19–$4.19**, standard deviation **$0.42** across 488 priced ZIPs. The earlier impression of compression came from eleven market-level medians, which smoothed away a two-dollar spread.

## Walmart's SC prices are tighter than Aldi's

| | n | median | range | sd |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Aldi (SC ZIPs) | 488 | $2.90 | $2.19–$4.19 | $0.42 |
| Walmart (SC stores) | 7 | $2.74 | $2.17–$3.72 | $0.45 |
| Walmart, excluding Williston | 6 | $2.71 | $2.17–$2.74 | **$0.22** |

Drop one store and Walmart's SC prices sit in a 57-cent band with a **$0.22** standard deviation — roughly half Aldi's dispersion. Walmart is the *more* consistent of the two in South Carolina.

Walmart undercuts Aldi in **six of seven** matched ZIPs, by $0.21 to $0.82. Williston is the only reversal.

## So the question is only about Williston

$3.72 is not a bad reading. It is **Walmart's third most common price point nationally** — 6 of 150 sampled stores — and it cleared the primary-item guard that rejects marketplace substitutions. The store is real and the price tier is real.

It is also a genuine outlier *for South Carolina*: $0.98 above the next-highest SC store in the sample, where the other six span 57 cents.

Walmart runs a wide national ladder — sampled state medians run from **$2.16 in Arizona to $5.09 in Pennsylvania**, a 2.4x range — and South Carolina normally sits near the bottom of it. Williston is priced as if it were on a different rung from the rest of the state.

## What I cannot tell you

Why that one store sits a rung up. With a single observation there is no way to separate a deliberate zone assignment from store format, from a transient promotion elsewhere in the state, from sampling luck. The store page does not expose its own format, only its neighbours'.

Two things would settle it, in order:

1. **Repeat observations of store 795.** One reading cannot distinguish a price tier from a fluke. Sampling until it is hit several times would.
2. **The rest of the CSRA.** Aiken has two Supercenters (Richland Ave W, Whiskey Rd) named as store 795's neighbours. If they price near $2.74, Williston is a store-level anomaly; if they price near $3.72, it is a regional zone and the Aiken market genuinely runs high.

Until then the honest statement is narrow: Walmart's SC milk pricing is tight and low, one sampled store departs from it sharply, and that store's price is a real tier rather than an artifact.
