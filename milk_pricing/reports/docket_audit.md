# Audit of the rulemaking record: was consumer incidence raised?

**Date:** 2026-09-12 · Branch `claude/walmart-milk-pricing-sc-m7zc99`
**Docket:** AMS-DA-23-0031 (national FMMO pricing hearing; 49 hearing days Aug 2023 – Jan 2024;
128 comments/exceptions on the proposed rule, due 13 Sept 2024)

**Two findings, pulling in opposite directions.**

1. **Yes — it was raised, formally and specifically.** The Milk Innovation Group, through counsel,
   filed exceptions objecting that the price increase falls on *"women, school children, WIC, and
   SNAP participants"* and would *"most negatively impact lower income consumers."* USDA's final
   rule never uses the word "consumer." **That is a fully formed APA failure-to-respond
   predicate.**
2. **But the Supreme Court has held that consumers cannot obtain judicial review of milk
   marketing orders at all.** *Block v. Community Nutrition Institute*, 467 U.S. 340 (1984).
   Review is confined to **handlers** who exhaust the statutory petition process. This is a
   larger obstacle than *Sandoval* and it reshapes who can bring anything.

## 2. What MIG actually filed

*MIG's Comments, Exceptions, and Objections to USDA's Proposed Rule* (AMS-DA-23-0031-0080,
posted 16 Sept 2024, 55 pages, filed through Davis Wright Tremaine LLP). Its numbered objections
include, verbatim:

> **2. The consumers bearing this price increase include women, school children, WIC, and SNAP
> participants.**
>
> **3. Retail cost increases for fluid milk of pennies to quarters—and likely more—will most
> negatively impact lower income consumers, making access to this highly nutritious food more,
> not less, difficult.**
>
> **4. USDA proposes increasing costs for fluid milk to schools, hospitals, and other
> institutions that face budget constraints.**

It argues the rule is *"contrary to public interest,"* criticises USDA's own Regulatory Economic
Impact Analysis, and objects that USDA *"deviat[ed] from the USDSS model"* and used *"current
Class I differentials as a floor,"* which *"artificially enhances fluid milk prices."*

**That last objection matters independently**: it is a contemporaneous, represented-party
allegation that USDA departed from its own cost model in a way that raised Class I values. That
is the same terrain as the memo's §6 alternative-schedule counterfactual, raised on the record by
a party with standing.

## 3. USDA's response

From `reports/cria_audit.md`, on the 53-page final rule (590,370 characters):

- "consumer", "consumption", "retail", "household": **0 occurrences**
- "WIC", "SNAP", "food stamp", "nutrition assistance": **0**
- "low-income", "incidence", "distributional": **0**
- Civil Rights Impact Analysis: **three sentences**, scoped to "FMMO participants", concluding no
  likely impact on "low and moderate-income populations"

**A represented party objected on the record that the rule would raise costs for WIC and SNAP
participants and lower-income consumers. The final rule then certified no impact on low- and
moderate-income populations in three sentences, without using the word "consumer" once.**

This is the strongest single fact in the engagement. It is not an inference from a statistical
gradient; it is a comparison of two documents.

## 4. The obstacle, and it is serious

MIG cites *Block v. Community Nutrition Institute*, 467 U.S. 340 (1984) for the narrow point that
"handlers are deemed to have similar interests to consumers." But *Block*'s actual holding is the
problem:

> Three individual consumers, a handler, and a nonprofit organization sued over a milk market
> order. The Supreme Court held that **"it is clear from the structure of the Act that Congress
> intended that judicial review of market orders ordinarily be confined to suits by handlers"**
> in accordance with the provisions expressly entitling them to review after exhausting
> administrative remedies.

So the AMAA's structure **precludes consumer suits challenging milk marketing orders**. For this
theory that is worse than *Sandoval*: it is not that a particular cause of action is unavailable,
it is that the challenger class is wrong.

## 5. What that implies about vehicle

| Route | Viability |
|---|---|
| Consumer/organisational APA suit challenging the Class I differential | **Foreclosed by *Block*** |
| **Handler petition under 7 U.S.C. § 608c(15)(A), then district court review** | The route *Block* leaves open. Requires a regulated handler as petitioner. |
| Administrative civil-rights complaint to USDA OASCR | Not judicial review of the order; *Block* does not obviously bar it. Untested here. |
| FOIA for the CRIA worksheet | Available now; establishes whether any documented analysis exists |
| Comments/exceptions in the next amendment cycle | Available; builds a record with the consumer constituency actually represented |
| Legislative / oversight | Unaffected by *Block* |

**The realistic reading: the party with judicial review rights is a handler, not a civil-rights
organisation.** MIG's members are handlers, and MIG has already made the consumer-incidence
argument on the record. An NAACP role is more plausibly amicus, coalition partner, administrative
complainant, or record-builder for the next cycle than lead plaintiff.

Counsel should verify all of this independently. *Block* is 42 years old and there is doctrine
since on preclusion of review; whether an administrative civil-rights complaint or a
procedural-CRIA theory escapes it is a question this analysis cannot answer.

## 6. Honest qualifications

- **MIG is not a consumer advocate.** It is a coalition of fluid-milk processors with a direct
  commercial interest in lower Class I prices. Its WIC/SNAP argument is self-interested. That does
  not defeat the APA point — a significant comment requires a response regardless of the
  commenter's motive — but it will be characterised that way, and there is no disinterested
  consumer voice on this record to point to instead.
- **Zero consumer-side organisations filed.** That is a gap in the record, and it cuts both ways:
  it strengthens "the affected population was unrepresented" and weakens "the agency was squarely
  confronted and ignored it."
- **A keyword-search caution, again.** My docket-wide search for `low-income` returned 0 hits,
  yet MIG's brief says *"lower income consumers"* — unhyphenated, so the search missed it. Earlier
  in this project a case-insensitive search for "WIC" returned 7 false hits from BRUNSWICK and
  WICHITA. **Neither the presence nor the absence of a keyword result should be relied on without
  reading the underlying document.** Every figure above that matters was confirmed against the
  source text.
