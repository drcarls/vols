CyberResilient × UpliftIQThe value case

NIS2 monitoring, made objective.

CyberResilient sells NIS2 monitoring and compliance recommendations. UpliftIQ measures the same organisations from the outside — objectively, and inside your own walls. Here's what each does, then the ways they work together.

01 · What CyberResilient does

Sells NIS2 monitoring & compliance recommendations

You help essential and important entities meet the Swedish Cybersecurity Act: monitor their NIS2 posture, surface where they fall short, and recommend what to fix — with reporting a board and a regulator can trust.

  • •Maturity monitoring — track NIS2 posture and progress over time.
  • •Compliance recommendations — what to fix, mapped to NIS2 (and ISO 27001 / NIST CSF / CIS).
  • •Risk register, continuity, audit-ready reporting — the workflow around the score.

The strength — and the ceiling — is that it works from what the customer reports. Fast and clean, but self-declared and gated on the customer engaging.

02 · What UpliftIQ does

An objective posture engine — passive, and on-premise

UpliftIQ measures an organisation's security posture from public signals only — DNS, mail policy, certificate logs, exposure, procurement. No questionnaire, no access to their systems. It runs as four capabilities, deployed inside your environment:

1 Observe
Measure any org's external posture — no input needed.
2 Reconcile
Compare observed reality to what was self-reported.
3 Cluster + Benchmark moat
Position an org against anonymized peers.
4 Recommend + Optimize moat
Prioritize fixes, learning from cross-customer outcomes.

On its own it's a sensor and a scorer. Its value shows up when it feeds your platform and your data.

03 · How they work together

The cases

1

Outbound outreach drives customers

UpliftIQ scores NIS2-scope orgs in bulk; the weakest are a prioritized outbound list. You reach out with the org's own observed score as the opener — "your external posture ranks bottom of your peer group, see the full picture free" — feeding sign-ups.

e.g. the run's bottom three: Skellefteå Kraft 2 · Jämtkraft 24 · Tekniska verken 26 — a warm list, ranked worst-first.

2

Enrich & fact-check a customer's data

For a customer, put observed evidence next to their self-report to verify or challenge it — turning self-declared monitoring into evidence-checked monitoring.

e.g. self-assessment rates email "adequate"; observed shows Skellefteå Kraft at DMARC p=none — spoofable, verified live.

Within bounds: only a consenting customer's own domains/assets, scoped to what they've authorised — never covertly.

3

Benchmark customers vs. anonymized data moat

Cluster customers by sector, size, and maturity, and show each where they stand against anonymized peers — a benchmark only you can produce, because it needs your corpus. Sharper with every customer.

e.g. 10 operators — Green Cargo 78 → Skellefteå Kraft 2, median 39 (observed layer shown; your customer data makes it richer).

4

Prioritize the fix under constraints moat

Given a customer's budget and staff, recommend the optimal set of actions to close the most NIS2 gaps or raise maturity most — recommendations learned from what actually worked across similar orgs, not a static rulebook.

e.g. "with 2 FTE-weeks: enforce DMARC → publish MTA-STS → assign a security owner — biggest maturity gain for the effort."

5

Continuous monitoring & drift alerts

Re-score passively between assessments and alert on regressions — new exposure, a policy that weakened, a new supplier. This is "NIS2 monitoring" made literal and always-on.

e.g. Stena Line & Transdev have no DMARC record today — flag the moment it changes, either way.

6

Sector / regulator intelligence new line

Aggregate anonymized posture across a sector into intelligence sold up — to MSB, a regulator, a ministry. Your government roots make you the natural provider.

e.g. 8 of 10 Swedish energy/transport operators run unenforced DMARC — a sector-level finding no single assessment reveals.
